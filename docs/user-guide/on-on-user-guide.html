Feature · History

From the Hash House

A handful of expatriates in pre-war Kuala Lumpur needed a cure for the weekend. Nearly ninety years later, the cure has kennels on every continent and a song for every occasion.

Words by the Harrier Central team

Hashing grew out of an old English schoolboy game, hare and hounds, sometimes called the paper chase. One runner sets off with a bag of shredded paper and lays a trail; the rest follow a few minutes later and try to catch them. In December 1938 a group of British colonial officers and businessmen in Kuala Lumpur, then the capital of the Federated Malay States, started running it on Monday evenings.

Their base was the Selangor Club Chambers, a residential annexe whose dining room served food so unvarying that its residents called it the Hash House. The runners took the name with them. The Hash House Harriers were led by A. S. Gispert, known as G, with Cecil Lee, Frederick “Horse” Thomson and Ronald “Torch” Bennett among the early pack.

War, and a second start

The Second World War stopped the runs. Gispert was killed in early 1942 during the fighting for Singapore. In 1946 the survivors started again in Kuala Lumpur, and in 1950 the club was registered as a society with a short list of objectives that hashers still quote. Kuala Lumpur's kennel is known to this day as the Mother Hash.

From one kennel to thousands

For twenty years there was one hash. Then Singapore started its own in 1962, and kennels followed across Southeast Asia, Australia and beyond, usually carried by somebody who had been posted abroad and missed it. The first InterHash, a worldwide gathering, was held in Hong Kong in 1978. Today there are kennels on every continent, from monthly full-moon runs to clubs that meet several times a week.

What has not changed

The essentials are the same as they were in 1938. A hare lays a trail with marks only the pack can read. The trail splits at checks and doubles back on false trails, so the fast runners have to search while the slower ones catch up. Everyone finishes together at the On Inn, where the circle hands out down-downs and new hashers earn their names.

Where Harrier Central fits

Harrier Central began as a phone app for one hash, written by a hasher in his spare time when the Flutter toolkit was still at version 0.8. It has grown into a platform with an app for iOS and Android, an admin portal, and a website for every kennel at hashruns.org. Its job is to do the paperwork so the Grand Master, the Hash Cash and the Religious Advisor can get on with the trail and the circle.

Speaking Hash

The app uses the words your kennel uses. If you are new, these are the ones you will meet first.

Kennel
A hash club. Most hashers follow several and belong to one or two.
Hare
The runner who sets the trail. Often there are two or three.
Pack
Everyone chasing the hare's trail.
On-On
The call that you are on the true trail. Also the name of the party after the run.
Check
A mark where the trail could go several ways. The pack spreads out until someone finds the true trail.
On Inn
The finish, usually a pub. PackTrack ends each trail here.
Circle
The gathering at the end, led by the Religious Advisor, where down-downs are given.
Down-down
A drink downed in the circle for a reason, real or invented.
Virgin
Someone at their first ever hash. Visitors are from other kennels.
Hash name
The name the circle gives you, usually after a few runs and never the one you would have chosen.
Mismanagement
The committee: Grand Master, Religious Advisor, Hash Cash, Hare Raiser, Hash Flash and the rest.
Hash trash
The written account of a run, usually more entertaining than accurate.
ArrowOn-on. You are on the trail; keep going this way.
CircleA check. The trail continues somewhere from here; search for it.
Three barsFalse trail. Go back to the last check and try another way.
CrossNot this way. Marks vary from kennel to kennel; your hares will brief you.

Chapter 01 · Identity & access

Getting started

No passwords to forget. Your phone is your key, and a six-letter invite code is your spare.

ForEvery hasherInAppWeb

Harrier Central signs your device in, not a username. When you register, your phone receives its own secret and uses it to prove who you are on every request. There is nothing to type at the trail head and nothing for anyone to guess.

You may already have an account without knowing it. Kennel admins often register members at their first run so their run count starts on day one. If that happened to you, use your invite code rather than creating a second account, so your history stays in one place.

How to sign in on a new phone

  1. Install Harrier Central from the App Store or Google Play.
  2. Choose I have an invite code.
  3. Enter the six letters and tap Get Started!
  4. Your kennels, runs and history load from the server.

Lost the code? Choose Find my account, enter your hash name or email address, and the app emails your invite code to the address on file. If the address is out of date, your kennel's admin can correct it.

Just looking? Guest mode lets you browse kennels and upcoming runs without an account. When you are ready to RSVP or check in, the app asks you to sign up.

Your profile holds your hash name, photo and home location. The home location is what “runs near me” measures from.

If you leave the platform, deleting your account removes your personal details and keeps the anonymous run records your kennel's history depends on.

Chapter 02 · Kennels, membership & permissions

Kennels & membership

Follow any kennel to see its runs. Join one to make your runs count. The roster shows who does what.

ForHasherKennel adminHash CashInAppPortal

Search for a kennel by name, city, region or country. Kennels can add their own search tags, so a search for “Scotland” finds the clubs that call themselves Scottish even where the map files them under a city.

Following a kennel brings its whole run history to your phone, not just the last few days, and puts its upcoming runs in your list. Following is your choice and costs nothing. Membership is the club's decision: you are a member while your membership date is in the future, and your Hash Cash keeps that date current when you pay.

Follow a kennel

  1. Open the Kennels tab and search.
  2. Open the kennel's page.
  3. Tap Follow. Its runs appear in your list within a few seconds.

Who does what

Every kennel has its Mismanagement: Grand Master, Religious Advisor, Hash Cash, Hare Raiser, Hash Flash and others. The kennel page lists them, so you know who to ask before you turn up without money.

Running the software is a separate job. A kennel admin may hold no office at all, and an office holder needs no admin rights. Either can grant a function, and a kennel can switch functions on or off against the platform's defaults.

A hare can edit their own run without becoming an admin of the club.

Kennel admins see the roster with each member's run count, standing and contact details: cards on a phone, a dense grid on a desktop. They can add a hasher who is not on the platform yet, or import a whole roster when a club moves over.

Chapter 03 · Runs & the calendar

Runs & the calendar

Every run from every kennel you follow, in one list. And for hare raisers, a run sheet that fills itself in.

ForHasherHareHare RaiserInAppPortalWeb

The Runs tab lists upcoming trails across all your kennels. Filter by kennel, date range or distance from you. A run you attended today stays at the top until the evening is over, so the post-run admin is never more than a tap away.

Past runs carry small icons for what they hold: a PackTrack trail with the number of runners who recorded it, photos, chat and down-downs. You can see at a glance which runs have something to look at, and the list looks the same with or without a signal.

Open a run for the whole story on one screen: the start on a map, the hares, the fees, the on-after, and who is coming. One tap opens the start in your own maps app.

Set a run (hare raisers and hares)

  1. From the kennel, add a run, or open an existing one to edit.
  2. Set the date and time, then drop a pin or search the address. The time zone follows the location.
  3. Add the hares, the description, the fees and an image.
  4. Tap Save, or Save and send to post it to WhatsApp and email it in one go (chapter 09).

Run numbers stay continuous on their own. Insert a run from the club's archive and the later numbers move up; a club with an unusual history can still set a number by hand.

Runs can flow into the club's Google Calendar. And a kennel that already lists its runs on its own website can give Harrier Central the address; the runs are read from that page several times a day and appear here without anyone typing them twice.

Chapter 04 · Attendance & check-in

RSVP & check-in

Tell the hares you are coming so they buy enough beer. Check in when you arrive, even in a field with no signal.

ForHasherHash CashInAppWeb

RSVP with Yes, Maybe or No on any upcoming run. Going away for a weekend of hashing? Answer several runs in one go. Once a run has happened, the RSVP buttons go away; there is nothing left to decide.

At the start

Arrive at the start around the right time and the app offers to check you in. Self check-in saves a queue at the Hash Cash, and at a free run the button simply says Check In. Starting a PackTrack recording also checks you in, so your RSVP, attendance and trail always agree.

Check in the pack (Hash Cash)

  • Search the list of members and recent visitors, or scan a hasher's QR code.
  • Check in a visiting group in one action.
  • Take the fee at the same time: cash, credit or card.
  • No signal? Carry on. Everything syncs when you reconnect.

Virgins and visitors are marked as they check in, so the circle can welcome them properly later.

Your check-in is what counts. A run you attended adds to your totals for that kennel the moment it syncs.

Chapter 05 · Live tracking

PackTrack

See where the pack is when you have lost it. Replay the whole run afterwards, photos and all.

ForHasherHareKennel adminInAppWeb

Start tracking from the run tools and put the phone in your pocket. PackTrack keeps recording with the screen off, stores points when you lose signal and sends them when you are back in range, and stops on its own once you have clearly finished, so it does not follow you home.

Choose Best, Balanced or Power Saver before you set off. All three use precise GPS; they differ in how often your phone reports your position, which is what decides the battery cost on a long trail.

Lost? Find the pack

  1. Open the live map from the run tools.
  2. Switch to Radar for the bearing and distance to each runner.
  3. Or open the List, sorted by trail length or by who is nearest.

Marks on the map

Hares can drop typed marks at their exact position: checks, drink stops, cautions with their own text, and the On Inn, which ends the drawn trail. A distress mark always shows, even if you have hidden that runner's trail.

Replay and share

After the run, scrub through the replay and watch the pack spread at each check. Photos pop up at the moment they were taken. Share the replay to anyone; it plays on the web without the app. Export your trail as GPX, or import one from your watch, Strava or Garmin.

Trail TV puts the live and recent trails on the big screen at the on-after.

Your recorded trail is cleaned for GPS noise rather than deleted, and time stood at a check is recognised as standing still, so jitter is not counted as distance.

Chapter 06 · Photos

Photos & Hash Flash

Take the photo on trail and it files itself under the right run, pinned to the right spot.

ForHasherHash FlashInAppPortalWeb

Photos you take from the run tools attach themselves to the run and to the place you were standing. For each one you choose: private, or submitted to the club. A full-quality copy also goes to your camera roll, so the club's copy is never the only one.

Already took them with the camera app? Import from your camera roll and Harrier Central works out which photos belong to this run, by time and place, and pins each where it was taken.

Review submissions (Hash Flash)

  1. Open the review queue for the run.
  2. Approve, reject or delete, one at a time or in bulk.
  3. Pick a cover photo to give the run card a face.
  4. Crop where needed. The original is kept, so a crop can be undone.

Approved photos appear as pins on the run map and in a carousel you can swipe through. Every photo is shown whole, at its own shape, so whoever was standing at the edge stays in the picture.

The public gallery on the web never shows GPS coordinates. Where people were stays private.

Chapter 07 · Circle, down-downs & songs

The circle

Charges, songs and the record of what really happened, all in the Religious Advisor's pocket.

ForReligious AdvisorHasherInAppWeb

The Religious Advisor records each down-down against a hasher with its reason, and the list becomes the circle's running order. Mark one done, undo it, or cancel it; a noisy circle forgives mistakes. A live count of drinks poured keeps the beer meister informed.

Any hasher can nominate somebody, checked in or not, so the circle is not only the RA's ideas.

Lead a song

  1. Open the kennel's songbook from the run.
  2. Select the song.
  3. Everyone at the run gets a notification and the words on screen.

Each kennel chooses the songs it actually sings from a shared library, so the songbook sounds like your club.

Hash trash

After the run, the hash trash records what happened. It sits on the run's page for those who were there to relive and those who were not to catch up on. Past down-downs show there too.

Chapter 08 · Money & the ledger

Hash Cash

One ledger for run fees, memberships, kit and credit. It works offline and never charges anyone twice.

ForHash CashHasherKennel adminInApp

Each run can charge members, visitors and virgins different amounts. The Hash Cash takes payment at check-in in cash, from a credit balance or by card, and can charge a visiting group in one transaction. A free run records attendance without pretending money changed hands.

Hashers can pay their own fee from their phone and renew their membership in the app, so nobody has to catch the Hash Cash between the circle and the bar.

What the ledger handles

  • Run fees by member, visitor and virgin price.
  • Memberships on annual, rolling or calendar-year renewal.
  • Haberdashery: shirts, badges and kit, reported separately.
  • Credit: pay for ten runs up front, if your kennel uses it.
  • Expenses with a photo of the receipt.

Money is held as exact decimals, so rounding never loses a cent. A payment taken without signal waits in an outbox and is sent when you reconnect, and a retried payment is recognised as the same payment.

Member standing is worked out from payment history every night. A lapsed membership shows as lapsed without anyone touching it.

Reports by run and by period make reconciling the bucket simple, and can be emailed to the Hash Cash on a schedule.

Chapter 09 · Messaging, notifications & email

Chat, alerts & email

A chat for every run and every kennel. And one button that tells the whole club about the next trail.

ForHasherHare RaiserKennel adminInAppPortalWeb

Every run has its own chat, so “where are you?” has an obvious place to go. Every kennel has one too, and there are rooms for office holders: Grand Masters, Hash Cash, Religious Advisors and kennel admins can talk to their counterparts in other clubs. Send photos and locations, reply to a message, react with a beer, or message a hasher directly.

Chats open at the newest message. The unread badge clears the moment you read, on every device you own.

Notifications

Choose which runs and kennels may notify you. You will hear when someone messages a run you are going to, when the RA picks a song, and before a run you said you would attend.

Save and send a run

  1. Edit the run and tap Save and send.
  2. Tick Post to WhatsApp for a ready-written notice with RSVP links.
  3. Tick Email hashers. Choose Use run description, or Write with AI and add a prompt such as “make it a Halloween story”.
  4. Use Preview to me and Who gets it before you send.

The email goes to members, followers and anyone who has RSVP’d to the run, unless they have turned run emails off. Every email carries the run's facts, one-tap I’m in and Can’t make it buttons, and an unsubscribe link that works first time.

No app? The links in the WhatsApp notice and the email work in a browser. Sign in with a six-letter code sent to your email, or a passkey on a device you have used before.

Chapter 10 · Stats & reporting

Stats & leaderboards

Your run count is the closest thing hashing has to a rank. Here is how it is kept.

ForHasherGrand MasterInAppWeb

Your profile shows your runs for each kennel and your total across all of them, plus a rolling count for the last twelve months. Runs from before your kennel joined Harrier Central can be added as a historical figure, and a count built partly from that history is marked as an estimate.

Your run history is a map and a list: every run you have done, the trails you recorded, and the kennels you have run with.

Leaderboards

  • Kennel: the people you actually run with.
  • Global: how the wider hash is doing.
  • By period: so a newcomer is not forever behind a thirty-year veteran.

Grand Masters see attendance trends to tell whether the club is growing, and can have a summary emailed after each run. Visitors can read a kennel's public stats page to judge how active it is before they turn up.

Chapter 11 · Public web presence

Your kennel on the web

Every kennel gets a website at hashruns.org, kept up to date by the runs you already enter.

ForVisitorKennel adminInWebPortal

Your kennel's site lives at hashruns.org/your-kennel. It has a landing page with the next run, upcoming and past runs, an about page that explains hashing to newcomers, the songbook and your stats. A kennel with its own domain can point it at the site and keep its established address.

Each run has its own page at hashruns.org/your-kennel/run-number, with its map, its PackTrack replay and its photo gallery, ready to share in a group chat with the run's image as the preview.

Make the site look like your club

  1. Open the web editor from the admin portal.
  2. Rearrange the blocks on any page. Changes show on the next load.
  3. Set your colours, logo and light or dark mode. Text stays readable whatever colours you pick.

The root of hashruns.org is a calendar of runs from every kennel, and each kennel's pages are written for search engines, so somebody searching for a hash in their town can find you.

Old links from the previous hashruns.org still work. They land on the matching new page.

Chapter 12 · Administration

The admin portal

Everything an admin can do on a phone, on a big screen, at portal.harriercentral.com.

ForKennel adminMismanagementPlatform adminInPortal

The portal is the desk version of the app's admin screens. Editing a run there works exactly as it does on the phone, including Save and send, the email preview and the list of who gets it. Any capability added to one is added to the other.

Sign in by confirming from the app on your phone. There is no separate portal password.

What you can do in the portal

  • Edit your kennel: details, prices, logo, songs and integrations.
  • Create, edit and email runs.
  • Manage the roster, offices and permissions.
  • Approve photos as Hash Flash.
  • Open the web editor for hashruns.org.

Starting a new kennel

A club that is not on Harrier Central yet can request a kennel at harriercentral.com. The request lands in the platform team's queue; once approved, the founder receives an emailed code and becomes the first admin.

Platform admins also see usage across the whole platform, can merge duplicate accounts so every run moves to one, and can publish newsflashes to targeted groups of hashers.
